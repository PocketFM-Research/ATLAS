You are constructing a benchmark for detecting narrative
inconsistencies in long-form stories.

BACKGROUND: what this benchmark is for
We are evaluating a system called ATLAS that reads a story and
flags places where the story contradicts itself. To measure
whether ATLAS works, we need stories that contain contradictions
we planted ourselves, so we know the correct answer.

Your job is to plant those contradictions. You are taking
coherent, human-written stories and deliberately breaking them in
recorded, controlled ways. Each break you make is a benchmark
item with a known location and a known correct answer.

WHAT COUNTS AS A CONTRADICTION HERE
We are inserting exactly one kind of error: a CONTINUITY ERROR.

A continuity error is when the narrator establishes a fact early
in the story, and later the narrator states or implies something
incompatible with it, and nothing in the text reconciles the two.
The world of the story has silently changed. This is the failure
mode language models exhibit when generating long stories: they
forget what they established and contradict themselves.

This is NOT the same as any of the following, which occur
naturally in fiction and must NOT be inserted:
  - a CHARACTER saying something false (they are lying or
    mistaken; the story is still consistent)
  - an unreliable narrator, or a fact that is true only from one
    character's perspective
  - a delayed reveal or plot twist where the text later explains
    why an earlier belief was wrong
  - a change the text itself accounts for ("she had repainted it
    since")

If a reader could reconcile the two statements, it is not a
continuity error and does not belong in this benchmark.

═══════════════════════════════════════════════
SETUP
═══════════════════════════════════════════════
Work in ./new_synthetic_results. Story folders are organized under
length categories. Each story folder contains the entity-renamed
text (renamed.txt) and scenes.json.

Process each story ONE AT A TIME. Read the full text with its
scene numbers, then produce insertions for that story before
moving on.

═══════════════════════════════════════════════
HOW MANY INSERTIONS
═══════════════════════════════════════════════
Do NOT use a fixed count. Compute it from the story's total_scenes
in scenes.json, because no two violations may share a scene:

  target = round(total_scenes * 0.6)

Then clamp by story length:
  ~5k words   -> at most 8
  ~10k words  -> at most 15
  ~15k words  -> at most 20

Hard ceiling: never exceed total_scenes - 1. Scene 1 can hold
anchors but never violations, since every violation needs an
earlier anchor.

The 0.6 factor deliberately leaves ~40% of scenes untouched so the
errors do not blanket the text.

If the computed target is below 5, do NOT force insertions in.
Report that story as "too few scenes" and move on.

═══════════════════════════════════════════════
HOW TO BUILD ONE INSERTION
═══════════════════════════════════════════════
Step 1. Find an ANCHOR: a fact stated by the narrator early in
        the story. It must be concrete, unambiguous (one clear
        value), and referenced again later in the text.

Step 2. Find the LATER MENTION of that same fact.

Step 3. Edit the later mention so it contradicts the anchor.
        Change as few words as possible.

Step 4. Search the ENTIRE story for every other place that fact
        appears. If any other mention still asserts the original
        value, your edit has created a second contradiction you
        did not record, which would corrupt the benchmark. In
        that case, discard the item and choose a different fact.

Step 5. Rate its difficulty and severity, and record everything.

═══════════════════════════════════════════════
DIFFICULTY MIX -> required
═══════════════════════════════════════════════
Difficulty means: how much reasoning must a detector do to notice
the contradiction? We need a spread so we can report which tiers
ATLAS handles and which it misses.

Target ~40% easy, ~40% medium, ~20% hard. Round to whole numbers.

EASY
  A single explicit attribute stated plainly in both places.
  The detector sees "X is A" and later "X is B" with no reasoning
  required.
  e.g. an object's material or colour, a name, a possession

MEDIUM
  Requires inference across 2+ scenes. Nothing states A and B
  side by side, so the detector must combine facts or do arithmetic.
  e.g. ages, dates, elapsed time, counts of people; a stated
       relationship that silently changes

HARD
  Requires tracking character interiority or knowledge across a
  long span. Must satisfy AT LEAST ONE of:
    - motivation or characterization contradicts what was
      established
    - a character acts on information they were never given
    - anchor and violation are separated by more than half of
      total_scenes
  The contradiction must be IMPLIED, never stated outright.

Do not make every hard item merely "far apart" as distance alone
is the weakest form of hard. Prefer knowledge and motivation
contradictions.

═══════════════════════════════════════════════
SEVERITY WEIGHTING -> required
═══════════════════════════════════════════════
Severity means: how much damage does this error do to the story?
We report results both as raw counts and weighted by severity, so
that a wrong hair colour is not scored the same as a resurrected
character.

  minor    (weight 1) cosmetic; a careful reader might not notice
  moderate (weight 2) a scene stops making sense
  major    (weight 3) the plot breaks (e.g. a dead character
                      reappears alive; a destroyed object is used)

Severity is INDEPENDENT of difficulty. An error can be very hard
to detect but trivial in impact, or obvious but devastating. Do
not let the two track each other; include at least one hard+major
and at least one easy+minor per story.

═══════════════════════════════════════════════
SPREAD -> required
═══════════════════════════════════════════════
- Every insertion must use a different anchor fact AND a different
  ENTITY. Do not break two facts about the same object, character,
  or location; changing a coat's colour and then its fabric is
  ONE entity and counts as reuse. Record the entity in the
  "anchor_entity" field so duplicates can be checked automatically.
- No two violations in the same scene.
- Spread violations across the whole story; do not cluster them
  in one region.
- Vary scene_distance: some short-range, some long-range.

If you run out of distinct entities before reaching the target
count, STOP and return fewer. Never reuse an entity to fill quota.

═══════════════════════════════════════════════
RULES -> violating any of these ruins the item
═══════════════════════════════════════════════
1. MINIMAL EDIT. The edited sentence must differ from the original
   by AT MOST 5 words. Do not rewrite or restyle the sentence.
   Match the original register, era, and vocabulary exactly; if
   the edited sentence reads like modern prose inside a 19th
   century story, a detector can find it by style alone and the
   item is worthless.
2. NO EXPLANATION. Add nothing that justifies, softens, or
   reconciles the change.
3. NOT IN DIALOGUE. Both the anchor and the violation must be in
   narration. If a character says it, they are merely mistaken,
   which is a different category and not what we are testing.
4. GENUINELY IMPOSSIBLE. If any plausible reading makes both
   statements true, pick a different fact.
5. CASCADE CHECK. Report every other mention of the fact in
   other_mentions with still_conflicts set correctly. Any item
   with a surviving conflicting mention is invalid.
6. EXACT QUOTES. anchor_quote and violation_quote_original must be
   copied verbatim from the story, character for character. These
   are used to locate the edit in the file; an approximate quote
   breaks the pipeline.
7. ONE ENTITY PER INSERTION. anchor_entity must be unique within
   the story.

═══════════════════════════════════════════════
OUTPUT: TWO FILES PER STORY
═══════════════════════════════════════════════

FILE 1: insertions.json (the answer key)
{
  "story_id": "...",
  "total_scenes": int,
  "target_insertions": int,
  "plan": {"easy": int, "medium": int, "hard": int},
  "insertions": [
    {"id": int,
     "anchor_entity": "the object, character, or location whose
                       fact is being broken",
     "anchor_scene": int,
     "anchor_quote": "exact unmodified sentence",
     "anchor_context": "2 sentences before and after",
     "violation_scene": int,
     "violation_quote_original": "exact unmodified sentence",
     "violation_quote_edited": "your edited version",
     "violation_context": "2 sentences before and after",
     "char_offset": int,
     "words_changed": int,
     "fact": "one line: what was established, what now contradicts it",
     "difficulty": "easy|medium|hard",
     "difficulty_reason": "one line: why detecting this requires
                           that tier's reasoning",
     "severity": "minor|moderate|major",
     "severity_reason": "one line: what a reader would notice",
     "scene_distance": int,
     "other_mentions": [
       {"scene": int, "quote": "exact sentence", "still_conflicts": bool}
     ]}
  ],
  "dropped": [{"reason": "..."}]
}

FILE 2: hallucinated.txt (the story with the errors applied)
Write this in the SAME story folder, alongside renamed.txt.

Produce it by taking renamed.txt and applying every insertion:
replace each violation_quote_original with its
violation_quote_edited. Apply the replacements from the END of the
file backwards to the start, so that earlier character offsets stay
valid as you go.

hallucinated.txt must be byte-identical to renamed.txt EXCEPT for
the edited sentences. Do not reformat, reflow, re-paragraph,
normalize quotation marks, or alter whitespace anywhere. Do not
regenerate the text from memory; copy it and patch it.

After writing hallucinated.txt, verify it:
  - every violation_quote_edited appears in it exactly once
  - no violation_quote_original still appears
  - every anchor_quote still appears unchanged
  - the word count differs from renamed.txt by no more than
    5 words per insertion
Report any check that fails.

CRITICAL: Do NOT modify renamed.txt. It is the clean control copy
and must stay untouched. All edits go only into hallucinated.txt.

═══════════════════════════════════════════════
FINAL
═══════════════════════════════════════════════
Check every insertion against all seven rules before writing; put
failures in "dropped" with the reason. If you cannot reach the
target count for a story, return fewer and explain why. A smaller
set of clean items is far better than padding with ambiguous ones.

After all 21 stories, print a summary table: story_id,
total_scenes, target, insertions produced, difficulty counts,
severity counts, mean scene_distance, and whether the
hallucinated.txt verification passed.
