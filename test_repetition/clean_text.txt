Scene 1: The Lighthouse

Marina climbed the spiral staircase to the top of the lighthouse just before dawn.
The ocean stretched flat and dark in every direction.
She checked the lamp, tightened the lens housing with a wrench, and noted the reading in her log.
A ship was visible on the horizon, its running lights blinking steadily.
She watched it pass and then descended to make coffee.

Scene 2: The Trial

Judge Okafor entered the courtroom and called the session to order.
The defendant, a man named Herrera, sat at the left table with his lawyer.
Prosecution presented three photographs and a witness statement.
"The fingerprints do not match," the defense attorney said firmly.
After forty minutes of argument, Judge Okafor called a recess.
Reporters outside the door typed quickly on their phones.

Scene 3: Harvest

By October the orchard was heavy with apples.
Tomás worked the eastern rows, setting each apple gently into the wooden crate.
His daughter ran between the trees counting fallen ones.
"Twenty-three," she announced.
"Don't eat the bruised ones," he told her.
At dusk they drove the crates to the cold storage barn and padlocked the door.

Scene 4: The Surgery

Dr. Reyes scrubbed in at six in the morning.
The patient had been brought in overnight with internal bleeding from a motorcycle accident.
The procedure lasted three hours and forty minutes.
When she finally pulled off her gloves, a nurse handed her a cup of water.
"Stable," she said, and sat down in the hallway with her back against the wall.

Scene 5: The Archive

The basement archive held boxes going back to 1921.
Librarian Priya spent the afternoon rehousing deteriorating folders into acid-free containers.
She found a photograph of the town square taken the year the building was constructed.
None of the faces were labeled.
She scanned the photograph at 600 dpi and uploaded it to the digital catalog.
The original went back into a new sleeve.

Scene 6: The Bridge Opening

The mayor cut the ribbon at eleven in the morning.
Traffic had been backing up on the old bridge for twenty years.
A crowd of about three hundred people watched from the walkway.
Children pressed against the railing and threw paper boats into the river below.
The engineer who had designed the span stood near the back, away from the cameras.
By noon the first buses were crossing.
