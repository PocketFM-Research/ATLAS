Scene 1: The Road Out of Town

Evan left his town early in the morning, though it didn’t feel very early. The sky was pale and undecided, like it hadn’t made up its mind yet.

He had a small bag, not very heavy, just enough to make it feel like he was going somewhere important.

“I won’t come back,” he said, even though no one was there to hear it.

The road stretched ahead of him, quiet and empty.

He kept walking, thinking about what might be waiting for him—something better, probably something better.

Scene 2: A Different Start, Same Road

By the time Evan reached the edge of town, he realized he hadn’t actually left yet.

The same houses stood behind him.

The same pale sky hung above.

He adjusted the strap of his bag—it felt slightly heavier now, though he couldn’t say why.

“I should go,” he muttered, like he hadn’t already said that.

The road stretched ahead of him again.

It looked familiar, but that didn’t make sense.

Still, he started walking.

Scene 3: The Traveler Who Pauses

Evan walked for what felt like a long time, though nothing really changed.

A tree appeared on the side of the road. He didn’t remember seeing it before, but it also felt like he had passed it already.

He stopped.

“Was that always there?” he asked himself.

There was no answer, just wind—or something like wind.

He shrugged and kept going, thinking again about leaving, about not coming back, about how this time it would be different.

He didn’t notice he had said that before.

Scene 4: The Same Thought, Slightly Different

The road bent slightly, or maybe it always had.

Evan slowed down. His bag slipped a little on his shoulder, heavier again.

“I packed too much,” he said, though he couldn’t remember what he had packed.

He thought about turning back.

“I won’t go back,” he added quickly, correcting himself.

That felt important.

Ahead, the road stretched on in the same quiet way.

Behind him, the town looked exactly as it had before.

Or maybe closer.

Scene 5: A Conversation That Feels Reused

There was someone standing by the road.

Evan hadn’t noticed when they appeared.

“Leaving?” the stranger asked.

“Yes,” Evan said.

“People always say that.”

Evan frowned. “I mean it.”

The stranger nodded, but not in a convincing way.

“You said that before.”

Evan hesitated. “No, I didn’t.”

But even as he said it, it felt like something he had already said.

The stranger stepped aside.

“The road’s still there,” they said.

Scene 6: Progress That Isn’t Progress

Evan walked faster now.

The tree appeared again.

This time, he recognized it immediately.

“That’s the same tree,” he said.

He turned around.

The stranger was gone.

The town was still there.

Not farther away. Not closer. Just there.

“That’s not how roads work,” Evan said, a little louder this time.

But the road didn’t respond.

It just stayed the same.

Scene 7: The Attempt to Change Things

Evan stopped walking.

“This is stupid,” he said.

So he sat down instead.

For a while, nothing happened.

Then he stood up again.

“Okay,” he said, as if starting over.

“I’m leaving. I won’t come back.”

He adjusted his bag.

It felt lighter now.

Or maybe he just wasn’t paying attention.

He started walking.

Scene 8: The Almost-Ending

The road finally changed.

There was something ahead—a gate, or an arch, or something that looked like an exit.

Evan smiled.

“I knew it,” he said. “I just had to keep going.”

He walked faster.

The sky looked a little brighter.

The air felt different.

For a moment, it actually seemed like a new place.

Scene 9: The Reset That Isn’t Quite the Same

Evan stood at the edge of town.

He blinked.

The gate was gone.

The road was behind him again.

The houses stood in front of him.

His bag felt exactly the same as it had at the beginning.

“I won’t come back,” he said automatically.

He paused.

“That… doesn’t make sense.”

But he adjusted the bag anyway.

And he started walking.

Scene 10: The Ending That Repeats Poorly

Evan walked down the road.

The sky was pale.

The road was quiet.

He thought about leaving.

He thought about not coming back.

He thought about how this time it would be different.

Ahead, something waited.

Or maybe it didn’t.

It was hard to tell.

He kept walking.

He kept walking.

He kept—

And the story didn’t quite end.

It just continued the way it always had, like it had already done this before, even if it tried not to repeat itself so obviously.
